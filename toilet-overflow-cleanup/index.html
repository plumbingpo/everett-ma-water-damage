Dealing With Toilet Overflow Cleanup in Everett

A toilet overflow looks like a mop job, but the water does not stay on the tile. Some slips beneath the baseboard and into the wall, and some drops through the opening around the drain pipe. Most important is what the water was carrying. If it came from the tank or the supply line, it is clean. Bowl water is not clean, so we handle it as contaminated from the start.

In Everett, we begin each overflow job by determining the category of water, which decides how the rest is done. With clean water, the job is extraction followed by drying. Gray or black water means protective gear, removal of soaked porous materials like carpet pad, and disinfection of every surface it touched. Finally we meter the subfloor, the bottoms of the walls and the space underneath, so no moisture gets left behind.

Right Now

What to Do Right Now

  1. Stop the WaterFind the shutoff valve behind the toilet and turn it clockwise as far as it goes. When the valve is frozen, remove the lid of the tank and hold the float up so filling stops.
  2. Do Not Flush AgainNo more flushing, and no running the washer, a shower or a sink. With a blocked drain line, whatever goes down a drain may return through the toilet.
  3. Stay Out of Contaminated WaterWhen there is waste in the water or it came out of the bowl, keep pets and children away, and put on waterproof shoes and gloves before stepping in it.
  4. Check the Room BelowIf the toilet is upstairs, go check the ceiling directly below it. Should you see drips or a sag, cut the power to the room and get belongings out from under it.
  5. Contain Clean WaterUse old towels to keep clean water from reaching carpet or hallways. Contaminated water should be left to a crew that has the right disinfectants and equipment.
Will Insurance Pay?

Insurance and Toilet Overflow Cleanup

Many homeowners policies cover a toilet overflow when it is sudden and accidental, such as a failed fill valve or an unexpected clog. Sewer or drain backup is often excluded without a backup endorsement on the policy, and an overflow from a blocked main line can fall under that exclusion. Months of leaking around the base of a toilet usually falls outside coverage. Your agent or the policy wording can tell you which situation you are in.

Common Causes

Why This Happens

  • Septic systems that are full or drain fields that are waterlogged, which send water back toward the house.
  • Blocked traps and drain lines, which leave the bowl nowhere to empty but onto the floor.
  • A main sewer line blockage that backs waste up through the lowest toilet.
  • Too much paper, wipes or a child's toy flushed and lodged in the trap.
  • Tree roots or a collapsed section in the sewer line that slows every drain in the house.
What Is at Risk

Where the Damage Spreads

Subfloor Saturation

Grout lines and the base of the toilet let water pass under vinyl and tile. Plywood below swells and delaminates, which can release the flooring glued to it.

Wet Vanity and Wall Bases

Water spreads to the vanity toe kick and the lower edge of the drywall, where it wicks upward. Paint may look normal even when the wall is damp several inches up from the floor.

Ceiling Below

In a second story bathroom, water travels down along the drain pipe and collects on top of the ceiling drywall below, which stains, sags and in some cases collapses.

Baseboard and Door Trim Damage

Sitting at floor level, door casings and baseboards wick water up from their lower edge. Since trim is usually fiberboard, it puffs up, cracks apart and stays deformed.

What We Do

What Happens After You Call

1

Identify the Category

Before any cleaning, our crew confirms whether this is supply water or water from the drain and bowl, and sets up containment and protective measures to match.

2

Extraction and Removal

Standing water is extracted first. Contaminated drywall, baseboards or carpet pad come out where necessary, and the toilet is lifted when water has reached beneath the flange and base.

3

Disinfect Surfaces

We clean the subfloor, fixtures, wall bases and floors, then treat them with antimicrobial products. For Category 3 jobs, disinfection comes first and drying equipment starts only afterward.

4

Dry and Verify

Air movers and dehumidifiers dry the bathroom and any affected room below. Our crew keeps metering the subfloor and the ceiling until both read normal again.
