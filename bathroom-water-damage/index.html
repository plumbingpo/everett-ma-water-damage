Bathroom Water Damage: What Everett Homeowners Should Know

Tile, tubs and fixtures are made to get wet on top, but what lies beneath them is not. When caulk cracks, grout wears thin or a toilet seal fails, a little water slips past with every shower and every flush. It collects in the subfloor and the wall behind the tile, out of sight. We help Everett homeowners by tracing the leak path, checking its reach with meters, and drying or removing whatever got wet.

Slow leaks are the norm here, which means what you find has typically been there longer, and gone further, than it seems. Subfloor under a toilet can be soft all the way through while the tile above still looks fine. Our crew maps the wet area using meters and a thermal camera so nothing extra gets torn out, and only spots that cannot dry where they are get opened.

Warning Signs

  • A toilet that rocks slightly, or dark staining around its base
  • Hours after anyone showered, the grout is still dark with moisture
  • A brown ring or sagging spot on the ceiling directly below the bathroom
  • A musty smell that comes back no matter how often the room is cleaned
  • On the wall beside the shower, baseboard that has swelled or paint that is peeling
  • The bottom panel inside the vanity has swelled, stained or bowed

How We Fix It

How We Handle Bathroom Water Damage

1

Find the Leak

Each fixture is tested separately, from the supply lines to the toilet, tub and shower, and thermal imaging and moisture meters show us where water went beneath tile and in the walls.

2

Open What Is Needed

Soft wall board, loose tile and subfloor that has rotted are removed. A toilet leak means contamination, so anything porous that will not come clean is removed.

3

Dry the Hidden Spaces

The subfloor, the framing in the walls and the space above the lower ceiling are dried with air movers and dehumidifiers. We apply antimicrobial treatment and confirm dry readings before anything is closed up.

4

Rebuild and Reseal

New subfloor and backer board go in, and we can coordinate the tile, toilet seal, caulk and ceiling work, meaning the path of the leak gets closed and not just hidden.

The Clock

The Damage Timeline

First days

Water Gets Past

A little water passes the failed grout or caulk every time someone showers. Nothing shows yet, but the subfloor edge and the board behind the tile are absorbing it.

First few weeks

Subfloor Stays Wet

Between showers, the wood below the tile has no time to dry out. Mold gets going in the wall cavity and on the floor's underside.

One to three months

Signs Start to Appear

Grout darkens, tiles loosen, and the caulk line turns black. Where a room sits underneath, its ceiling develops a stain that gradually spreads.

Six months and beyond

Structure Weakens

Joists can begin to rot, and the subfloor near the toilet or tub becomes soft. Drying alone no longer fixes it, because carpentry and new tile are now part of the repair.

Behind the Surface

What Hides Behind It

Mold Behind Tile

By themselves, grout and tile do not keep water out. When the layer behind them stays wet, mold grows on the wall board where cleaning never reaches.

Poor Ventilation

Steam lingers after each shower when the exhaust fan is weak or absent. It condenses on the ceiling and walls, encourages mold, and keeps already wet materials from drying.

Leaking Shower Pan

Every use of the shower sends water beneath its floor when the pan or liner is cracked. Up top everything looks fine, yet the framing underneath never dries.

Rotted Floor at the Toilet

A failed wax ring leaks a little with every flush. It is category 3 water, and the wood surrounding the flange decays until the toilet wobbles.
